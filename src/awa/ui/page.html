Set up

awa needs an AI model to work, and access to the systems your process uses. What you enter is saved in the file .env in your project folder, on this computer only. awa never shows a saved value again; it only tells you whether it is set.

What happens here: you tell awa which AI model to use and how to reach your external systems. Everything is saved on this computer only, in the file .env of your project folder.

Project folder:

AI model

One provider is enough. Enter the key of the one your organization uses.

External systems

Systems awa can connect to. To design your process on a database or an API, add it under Your data, External systems: there awa tests it and reads its tables or operations. Here you only enter settings; everything else can wait.

Your data

Show awa what your process works on: the files it reads, and the external systems (databases, APIs) it reaches. awa then designs the process on your real data instead of inventing some.

What happens here: a file is copied into your project and read there; your original is never changed. A system is tested with one read-only call, its settings are saved in the file .env on this computer only and never shown again, and awa reads its structure (tables and columns, or the operations of an API), not its rows unless you allow example rows. You can skip this step.

Start from an example

Opens a shipped example in a new project folder, the way you would start your own process: its data files are copied under Files and its process description is filled in on Describe. Its eval cases are on Test. Your current folder is not changed.

Files

    External systems

    A database or an API your process reads. The design then uses its real tables or operations, live and read-only, and the test runs read it too. Systems you do not add here get a stand-in: a small local copy with sample records awa writes from your description.

    Describe your process

    Write down in your own words what happens: what comes in, who checks or decides what, which systems are involved, and what should come out at the end. A few sentences are enough. awa designs the agents, eval cases and connection steps from it.

    What happens here: awa reads your description, writes eval cases first, designs the agents against them and repairs the design in a few rounds, until the cases pass or the limits are reached. The cases come from your description, so you must still check them. This usually takes a few minutes; the result shows whether the target was reached.

    Suggest patterns

    Not sure how the agents should work together? awa suggests up to three ways from your description, and can test them side by side.

    Progress

      Details

        Review

        What awa designed for your process. Check that it matches how the work is really done.

        What happens here: you check what awa designed before anything runs: who does what, which systems are involved, and which eval cases exist. You can change it.

        Connect

        Connect the systems that still work on a stand-in, so the complete result runs on your real data. A stand-in is a small local copy with sample records awa wrote from your description, so you can test before connecting.

        What happens here: for each system awa shows what it needs. Keys are saved in the file .env on this computer and never shown again. Test connection makes one small, read-only call; Save and connect saves, tests and then switches the system from the stand-in to the live system.

        Test

        awa runs every eval case against your system and checks the result. Systems that are not connected yet answer from their stand-in, so you can test before connecting them.

        What happens here: awa runs every eval case against your system and shows which ones passed and why a failed one failed. You can change what a case sends (and so which of your files it uses) and run it again.

        Try it

        Talk to your system the way you would talk to a colleague. Before a step that is marked 'asks you first' (see Review), it shows you what will happen and asks you here. Check that list before you rely on it.

        What happens here: you ask, the system works, and you see each step it takes in plain words. Steps marked 'asks you first' on the Review page wait for your OK. Everything else runs without asking.

        Use it

        Put the system to work: see what starts it by itself, try it once on a sample, follow what it did and decide what waits for your OK. Your system is a folder of plain files: download it to hand it to your IT team.

        What happens here: you see whether the system is running, what starts it, the runs so far and the approvals that wait for you, and you learn the one command that keeps it running after you close this window.

        Download the project (.zip)

        The download never contains your .env file with the saved keys.

        Approvals

        Runs of this project that wait for a person. Your answer is saved in the project folder; the running awa serve or awa worker continues the run, or you continue it here when nothing runs.

        What happens here: runs that started by themselves and wait for a person show up here as cards: what will happen, the values, and why you are asked. The same cards are on the Use it step.

        Hand to a coding assistant

        A written brief for a coding assistant: your process, the agents, ready prompts, templates and the commands that check the result. Copy it into the assistant, or save it as BRIEF.md in your project folder.

        What happens here: the brief is written from the files in your project folder, so it always matches what exists. It never contains the values of your settings. Create or change your system first; then come back to get a new brief.
