# Contract Clause Checklist

A generic checklist of clauses to locate and describe. It contains no negotiation
positions: those come from the organization's playbook. Findings are for review by a
qualified lawyer and are not legal advice.

## Parties and scope

| Clause | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Parties | Legal names, entities, and signatories match the intended ones |
| Definitions | Key defined terms and anything unusually broad or narrow |
| Scope | What is supplied, deliverables, service levels, acceptance |
| Order of precedence | Which document wins when documents conflict |

## Money

| Clause | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Price and fees | Amounts, currency, basis, what is included |
| Price changes | Indexation, increases, notice periods |
| Payment terms | Due dates, invoicing, late payment interest, set-off |
| Taxes | Who bears which taxes |

## Risk allocation

| Clause | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Warranties | What is promised, for how long, remedies |
| Indemnities | Who covers which third-party claims, any caps |
| Limitation of liability | Caps, exclusions, carve-outs from the cap |
| Insurance | Required cover and evidence |
| Force majeure | Events covered, notice, termination right |

## Information and property

| Clause | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Confidentiality | Scope, duration, permitted disclosures |
| Intellectual property | Ownership of deliverables, licenses granted, background IP |
| Data protection | Personal data processed, roles, security, transfers, sub-processors |
| Security and audit | Security requirements, audit and inspection rights |

## Term and exit

| Clause | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Term and renewal | Start, duration, automatic renewal, notice to stop renewal |
| Termination | For convenience, for cause, notice periods, cure periods |
| Consequences of termination | Transition help, data return or deletion, fees due |
| Assignment and change of control | Whether either side may transfer the contract |

## Other obligations to flag wherever they appear

- Exclusivity, non-compete, or most-favored customer terms.
- Non-solicitation of employees.
- Unilateral right to change terms or policies by reference.
- Minimum volumes or purchase commitments.
- Governing law, jurisdiction, and dispute resolution.
- Notices: how and where formal notices must be sent.
