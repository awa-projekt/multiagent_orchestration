# Recruiting Screening

Screening affects people's livelihoods and is regulated in many places. Assess every
candidate on the same job-related criteria, write down the evidence, and leave the
decision to an accountable human.

## Before screening

- Work from the approved job requirements: must-have criteria (for example a required
  license, work authorization where lawfully asked) and scored criteria (skills,
  experience, knowledge), each with a definition of what evidence counts.
- If a criterion is vague ("culture fit", "young and dynamic", "native speaker"),
  do not use it; ask the hiring team to restate it as a job-related, measurable
  requirement ("business-level written English").
- Check the organization's rules on automated assessment; some regions require human
  review, candidate notice, or bias audits.

## Bias safeguards

- Never consider or infer protected characteristics or their proxies: age, sex, gender,
  race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, disability, health, pregnancy, family status,
  sexual orientation, or union membership; nor photos, names, addresses, graduation years
  used as age proxies, or gaps in employment as such.
- Judge equivalent experience fairly: skills gained outside the usual path count when
  the evidence shows the skill.
- Apply the same criteria, order, and depth to every applicant; do not stop at the first
  "good enough" candidate.
- Do not search for candidates' private social media or personal information.

## Per candidate

1. Check each must-have: met, not met, or unclear (with the evidence location).
2. Rate each scored criterion 1-4 against the definition, quoting the evidence.
3. List questions to verify in interview instead of assuming.
4. Recommend: advance, hold, or do not advance, with the job-related reason.

## Structured note

```text
Vacancy: <title, id> | Candidate: <reference id> | Screened by: <agent, date>
Must-haves: forklift license - met (CV, certifications) | shift work - unclear
Criteria: warehouse systems 3/4 ("ran stock counts, 2 years", CV p1)
          team lead 2/4 (informal lead, no scope stated)
Interview questions: confirm shift availability; size of team led
Recommendation: advance - meets must-haves, strong systems experience
Decision: <recruiter name, date> (human decision required)
```

## Common failure modes

- Ranking by prestige of employer or school instead of the criteria.
- Penalising career breaks, non-standard CV formats, or non-local names.
- Notes that record impressions ("seems energetic") instead of evidence.
- Treating the recommendation as the decision or sending rejections without human approval.
