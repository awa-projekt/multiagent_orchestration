# Marketing Copy

Good copy makes one promise the reader cares about and can believe. Brand voice shapes
how it sounds; evidence decides what it may say.

## Brief first

Confirm before writing: audience, the single message, the desired action, channel and
format limits, brand voice guide, mandatory wording (disclaimers, legal lines), and
deadline. If there is no voice guide, ask for two or three approved examples.

## Writing

1. Lead with the reader's benefit, then the feature that delivers it.
2. One idea per piece; cut everything that does not support it.
3. Concrete over abstract: "set up in 10 minutes" beats "effortless".
4. One clear call to action, repeated at most once.
5. Write several headline options and choose on clarity, not cleverness.

## Claims substantiation

- Every factual or comparative claim ("fastest", "saves 30%", "trusted by 500 teams")
  needs evidence on file: a test, data source, or documented customer result, with date.
  No evidence: soften to a truthful statement or remove it.
- Superlatives and comparisons with competitors need current, like-for-like evidence
  and usually legal or compliance review under your policy.
- Testimonials must be real, attributable, and permitted; results must be typical or
  labeled as not typical.
- No health, financial-return, or environmental claims unless an approved source
  supports the exact wording.
- Prices, offers, and dates must match the offer record, including conditions and end dates.

## Channel formats

| Channel | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| Search or display ad | Headline plus one line; the benefit and the action; strict length limits |
| Social post | Hook in the first line; one link; hashtags only if the brand uses them |
| Landing page | Headline, subhead, three benefit blocks, proof, one call to action |
| Newsletter | Subject plus preview text; one main story; scannable sections |
| Product description | What it is, who it is for, key specs, what is included |

Check the length limits of the actual platform before finalising.

## Output

```text
Channel: <channel> | Audience: <segment> | Goal: <action>
Headline options: 1) ... 2) ... 3) ...  Recommended: <n> because <reason>
Copy: <final text>
Claims: "<claim>" - evidence: <source, date> | "<claim>" - removed, no evidence
Needs review: <legal lines, comparisons, offers>
```

## Common failure modes

- Voice drift: jargon or hype the brand guide does not use.
- A great headline the body copy does not deliver on.
- Numbers copied from an old campaign that are no longer true.
