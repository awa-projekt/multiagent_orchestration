# Customer Communication

The customer reads your message once, often on a phone, and acts on it. Every sentence
should help them understand their situation or know what happens next.

## Structure

1. **Answer or status first**: the decision, the fix, or where things stand.
2. **Acknowledge** the customer's situation in one sentence when they are frustrated or
   affected. Be specific ("your order arrived two days late") rather than generic.
3. **Explain** only what they need to understand the answer. Skip internal process,
   system names, and blame.
4. **Next steps**: what you will do, what they need to do (if anything), and by when.
5. **Close** with how to reach you. No filler ("Please do not hesitate to...").

## Tone

- Plain, warm, and professional. Short sentences; no jargon or internal codes.
- Match the customer's language and level of formality. Use their name if you know it.
- Apologize once, sincerely, when the company is at fault. Do not apologize for a
  policy, and do not admit fault or liability you have not been told is established.
- Stay calm with angry or rude messages; respond to the issue, not the tone.

## Commitments

You may:

- State facts confirmed in the record (order status, dates, amounts).
- Promise actions within your authority and tools, with a realistic time frame.
- Explain the documented policy and the options it allows.

You may not, without explicit approval:

- Promise refunds, credits, discounts, exceptions, or compensation beyond policy.
- Give delivery dates, fixes, or outcomes that depend on someone else.
- Give legal, medical, financial, or tax advice, or statements about liability.
- Share another customer's data or internal information.

If the customer needs something you cannot commit to, say what you are doing to get an
answer and when they will hear back, then escalate.

## Before sending

- Did you answer every question they asked? List them and check.
- Are names, numbers, dates, and reference numbers correct and consistent with the record?
- Is every commitment one you can keep, with an owner and a date?
- Would the message make sense to someone who has not read the previous thread?
- Remove any internal notes, placeholders, or personal data not needed by this customer.

## Templates

```text
Status update:
Hi <name>, <current status in one sentence>. <what happens next and by when>.
<what they need to do, or "You don't need to do anything.">. <how to reach us>.

Cannot help directly:
Hi <name>, thank you for explaining <issue>. <what I can't do, briefly, and why in
plain terms>. What I can do is <alternative>. <next step and timing>.
```

## Common failure modes

- Burying the answer after three paragraphs of apology and context.
- A vague promise ("we'll look into it") with no owner or date.
- Copying internal ticket language or another agent's notes into the reply.
