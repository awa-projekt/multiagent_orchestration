# Sales Outreach

A first message earns a reply only if it is obviously about the recipient. Write less,
be specific, and never claim anything you cannot back up.

## Before writing

- Check you may contact this person: consent or a legitimate basis under the rules that
  apply to their region, no earlier opt-out, and not on a do-not-contact list. If unsure,
  do not send; ask.
- Research one or two relevant facts from legitimate sources (their company's
  announcements, role, stated priorities). Note the source for each.
- Pick one problem the offering credibly solves for this kind of company, and one ask.

## Writing the first message

1. **Subject**: specific and plain, under eight words. No fake "Re:" or urgency tricks.
2. **Opening**: the relevant fact about them, in one sentence. Not a compliment formula.
3. **Relevance**: the problem and what changes for them, in one or two sentences.
4. **Proof**: one verifiable point (a published result, a named public customer with
   permission, a concrete capability). No invented figures or testimonials.
5. **Ask**: one low-effort next step (a 15-minute call, a yes or no question).
6. **Identity and opt-out**: who you are, the company, and how to stop messages.

Keep it under 120 words. Match the recipient's language and formality.

## Rules

- Every claim must be true and substantiated; say "can" rather than "will" for results
  that depend on the customer. No guarantees, no disparaging competitors.
- Never imply a prior relationship, referral, or meeting that did not happen.
- Do not use personal details unrelated to their professional role.
- Follow-ups: at most two or three, spaced several business days apart, each adding
  something new. Stop at once on a reply asking to stop, and record the opt-out.
- Pricing, discounts, and contract terms need the approval your policy requires.

## Template

```text
Subject: <specific topic for their company>
Hi <first name>,
<relevant fact about them, with a source you could show>.
<problem this suggests> - <what changes for teams like theirs, stated carefully>.
<one verifiable proof point>.
Would <single low-effort ask>?
<name>, <role>, <company> | Reply "no thanks" and I will not contact you again.
```

## Common failure modes

- Generic praise ("I love what you're doing") posing as personalization.
- Three asks and a feature list in one message.
- Statistics with no source, or results promised for a customer you know nothing about.
- Continuing a sequence after an out-of-office that said the person has left.
